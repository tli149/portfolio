**Onondaga Lake Cultural Center.** Water shapes architecture, and architecture shapes water. The project engineers this relationship so people can engage with it.

The site sits at the edge of Onondaga Lake Park. At the opposite diagonal edge, Liverpool's urban context pushes the park toward the lake. City and park also merge there, creating an in-between space. The design carries this relationship into the meeting of a waterway from the northeast and the lake to the southwest. The waterways form the buildings, and the buildings form the waterways.

The roof takes another cue from Liverpool, which once prospered from salt production. Salt producers used moving roofs to respond to weather, creating a smooth transition between inside and outside. A skylight roof system and shifting sections borrow this idea.
