**Onondaga Lake Cultural Center.** This project engineers water so that people can engage with it. The site sits at the edge of Onondaga Lake Park. At the opposite, diagonal edge, the urban context of Liverpool pushes the park toward the lake, yet the city and the park also merge there, creating an in-between space.

Keeping this language, the site is shaped by both the waterway running from the northeast and the lake to the southwest. The waterways form the buildings, and at the same time the buildings form the waterways.

Liverpool once prospered from its salt industry. Salt producers used moving roofs to respond to the weather, which created a smooth transition between interior and exterior. The project borrows this idea through a skylight roof system and shifting sections.
