The proposal treats temporary housing as a flexible, open community dwelling. The traditional pol house of Ahmedabad, India, provides the elements for this reworking of student life.

Technology and online classes are changing how younger people live and study. The housing responds to those changes while keeping elements essential to the pol typology. The block is envisioned as a populated, lively community where residents interact with one another, the surrounding community, and the informal economies that circulate onto the site.

Digital technology makes physical presence less central and puts temporary programs in users' hands, rather than defining them through architectural spaces. Changeable interiors give two module types—type 1 for housing and type 2 for commerce—multiple uses in a rapidly changing society. Their main difference is the inner garden, drawn from the pol house as an air tunnel that releases hot air from the house and loosely links public and private spaces. Each residential module has its own, while combined commercial modules form a larger shared garden to suit their collective character.

Wood is the main material because it is light and suited to humid climates. Frames of wooden columns and beams take the place of traditional walls as guides that separate units and programs, allowing more spatial flexibility.
